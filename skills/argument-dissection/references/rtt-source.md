# RTT Source Notes: Argument Dissection

Based on the RTT Argument Dissection method from the provided RTT materials.

Core goal: evaluate proposals, papers, presentations, or verbal arguments to reveal weaknesses, biases, logical fallacies, missing information, and evidence problems.

Use when evaluating an argument or considering the validity of information.

Method summary:

Ask questions such as:

1. Does this argument address the real problem?
2. What is the point of view of the person or group making it?
3. Are there vague, ambiguous, or loaded words?
4. Are there value conflicts?
5. What descriptive or prescriptive assumptions are present?
6. Are there hints of bias?
7. Are there logical fallacies?
8. How good is the evidence?
9. If statistics are used, do they have proper context?
10. What information is missing?
11. Is the argument based on intuition or gut feeling?
12. Are analogies appropriate?
13. Are there rival causes or plausible alternative hypotheses?
14. Could a different conclusion be drawn from the same evidence?
15. What are the implications of accepting the argument as stated?

Meeting adaptation: identify the argument quality concern most likely to affect the decision and turn it into one host-readable question.
