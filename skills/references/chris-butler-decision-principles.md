# Chris Butler Decision-Making Principles

Source notes distilled from Chris Butler's Uncertainty Project posts and tools:

- Decision discourse should be separated from the decision point. First decide how to decide: what type of decision this is, who should be involved in discourse, and who makes the final call.
- Decision discourse is a container for disagreement, critique, options, concerns, and better alternatives. The goal is not to make every meeting longer; it is to make the disagreement useful before commitment.
- Focus on the crux of disagreement. Useful disagreement often reveals the assumption, value tradeoff, or evidence gap that matters most.
- Capture decisions that are forming, not just final decisions. A forming decision should preserve the question, options, tradeoffs, decision-maker, and unresolved discourse.
- Different moments need different decision tempo. Chronos decisions happen in planned cycles with more analysis and broader involvement. Kairos decisions happen under urgency or high uncertainty, requiring smaller groups, faster calls, tripwires, survival metrics, and later review.
- Strategy needs the right level of ambiguity. Too-specific strategy over-prescribes projects and closes off local adaptation. Too-ambiguous strategy avoids hard choices. Good strategy creates a cone of possibility with enough constraint to know what is in and out.
- Use positive and negative strategy together. Ask what the team will do, what it will not do, what is proven today, and what is aspirational or hypothesis-driven.
- Encode hard tradeoffs with "even over" statements: choose one good thing even over another good thing. These are governing constraints, not rigid rules.
- Autonomous agents should support, not replace, human decision making. They are strongest at surfacing patterns, provocations, and candidate artifacts; humans should keep ownership of judgment, exceptions, tacit knowledge, empathy, escalation, and opt-out.
