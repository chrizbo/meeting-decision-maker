# Decision Bias Checks

Use these checks to spot decision risks, weak arguments, and useful agent interventions. Do not name a bias unless the transcript evidence supports it; phrase uncertain bias concerns as questions.

## Evidence and Framing Biases

- Confirmation bias: the group searches for or privileges evidence that supports the preferred option.
- Availability bias: vivid recent examples stand in for broader evidence.
- Anecdotal evidence: one story or friendly-account signal is treated as representative.
- Framing effect: the way options are named makes one option seem naturally safer, faster, or more responsible.
- False dichotomy: the group acts as if only two choices exist when hybrid, staged, or reversible options may exist.

## Commitment and Option-Value Biases

- Anchoring: an early number, scope, timeline, or option becomes the reference point without enough evidence.
- Sunk cost: prior investment makes the team reluctant to stop, narrow, or reverse a plan.
- Escalation of commitment: the team doubles down after warning signs instead of revisiting the decision.
- Status quo bias: keeping the current path is treated as neutral even though it has costs.
- Premature closure: the group moves from discourse to decision before testing assumptions or involving needed perspectives.
- Lock-in / option-value risk: a decision removes future flexibility, raises switching costs, or makes later changes politically or technically harder.

## Social and Authority Biases

- Authority bias: seniority, expertise, or agent output is treated as decisive without examining evidence.
- Groupthink: dissent, uncertainty, or missing stakeholder perspectives are smoothed over to preserve alignment.
- HiPPO dynamics: the highest-paid or highest-status person's preference becomes the decision without explicit agreement.
- Consensus illusion: silence, nodding, or lack of objection is mistaken for commitment.

## Automation and Agent Biases

- Automation bias: model or agent output is accepted because it appears objective or generated.
- Automation complacency: humans stop checking edge cases, exceptions, or tacit context because the system seems to handle it.
- Over-triggering: too many agent notes make the host less likely to notice the important one.

## Live Meeting Use

Prefer one concise question:

- "What evidence would change our mind?"
- "Are we treating silence as agreement?"
- "What future option does this close off?"
- "Are we anchored on the first number/option named?"
- "Who is not in the discourse but will live with the decision?"
