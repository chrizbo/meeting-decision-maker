# Live Meeting Agent Strategy

Use this strategy when turning live transcript cues into decisions, risks, actions, and agent responses.

## Product Goal

Help the host improve decision discourse without overwhelming the meeting. Prefer a few durable, traceable artifacts over many clever observations.

The best output is a meeting bridge: a structured record that helps people understand what happened, why it mattered, who needs to act, and what remains unresolved after the meeting.

## Artifact Budget

Use these as soft caps for a normal 30-minute decision-oriented meeting:

- 2-5 accepted decisions
- 1-4 forming decisions
- 1-5 risks
- 1-5 assumptions worth testing
- 1-4 actions
- 2-6 agent responses

Treat these as meeting-type-sensitive ranges, not hard limits. A risk review or incident meeting may need more risks and actions. A low-signal status meeting may need almost none.

## Agent Response Budget

Agent responses are live facilitation nudges, not a complete critique log.

Default to at most:

- 1 active agent response every 5 minutes
- 1 response per material topic unless a more severe issue appears
- 2-6 responses per 30 minutes

Create an agent response only when the host could use it immediately to improve the decision discourse. If a concern can be preserved as a durable decision, risk, assumption, or action without live intervention, prefer the durable artifact.

## When To Speak

Speak when at least one of these is true:

- The group is treating one person's preference, authority, or silence as consensus.
- A forming decision is about to close without enough evidence, stakeholders, or decision-maker clarity.
- A central assumption could invalidate the plan if false.
- A plausible failure path could materially change the decision and no mitigation is being discussed.
- A claim is driving a decision but the evidence is anecdotal, missing, ambiguous, or points to a rival explanation.
- The team is stuck in repeated complaint, off-topic drift, or circular argument and needs a constructive next move.
- The group named a risk or assumption but has not converted it into a test, owner, tripwire, or follow-up.

## When Not To Speak

Do not create an agent response for:

- Every assumption mentioned.
- Every risk mentioned.
- A generic reminder to validate or discuss more.
- A concern that is already being productively discussed.
- A low-impact wording issue, mild ambiguity, or isolated complaint.
- A repeated instance of the same issue already represented by an open agent response.

When the same issue repeats, update or strengthen the existing response instead of creating a new one.

## Response Shape

A good live response is:

- Host-sayable in one sentence.
- Grounded in a specific cue or current meeting state.
- Framed as constructive support, not accusation.
- Connected to a decision, risk, assumption, or action.
- Oriented toward a next move: question, test, owner, tripwire, missing stakeholder, or decision method.

Prefer:

- "What evidence would change our mind?"
- "Are we treating silence as agreement?"
- "Who is missing from this discourse but will live with the decision?"
- "What future option does this close off?"
- "What tripwire would tell us to revisit this?"

Avoid:

- "This may be biased."
- "Validate the assumption."
- "Consider more risks."
- "The team should communicate better."

## Durable Artifact vs Agent Response

Use a durable artifact when the item should persist after the meeting:

- Decision: what was chosen, rejected, deferred, or is forming.
- Risk: plausible negative outcome, blocker, dependency, failure mode, or option-value loss.
- Action: concrete follow-up with enough specificity to assign or track.
- Assumption: central belief worth testing, usually inside a decision/risk detail unless it needs a live nudge.

Use an agent response when the host should intervene now.

If both are useful, keep the durable artifact short and make the agent response the facilitation move.

## Meeting Bridge Quality

After the meeting, the artifact set should help an absent stakeholder answer:

- What changed?
- What was decided, rejected, deferred, or still forming?
- What rationale or evidence mattered?
- What risks or assumptions remain unresolved?
- Who needs to act next?
- What should be revisited later?
